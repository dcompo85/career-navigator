# Transparent scoring

These rubrics are decision aids designed for this package. They are not validated psychometric tools, labor-market forecasts, ATS simulations, or hiring probabilities. A high score does not guarantee eligibility or an offer.

## Shared scoring method

1. Confirm the user's requirements and preferences. Agree weights once for the current comparison set; defaults below sum to 100. Record changes and re-score existing comparisons if needed.
2. Rate each dimension from 0 to 4 using the anchors below, or U for unknown. Attach a short evidence note and source to every rating.
3. Weighted points = dimension weight × rating / 4.
4. Coverage = sum of weights for rated dimensions. With unknowns, report **earned points / assessed possible points**, coverage, and a possible total range from earned points to earned points plus unknown weights. Do not normalize a partial assessment into a confident score out of 100.
5. Show a single total out of 100 only at full coverage. Round totals to whole numbers; avoid false precision. A numeric rating is still a judgment, not a measured fact.
6. Confidence is High, Moderate, or Low, with a reason: source quality, relevance, recency, agreement, and important assumptions. Coverage measures how much is assessed; confidence measures how well it is supported.

Example: dimensions totaling 80 points are rated and earn 62. Report **62/80 assessed; 80% coverage; possible total 62–82/100**. Do not label it 78/100. Do not rank incomplete candidates solely by earned points.

| Rating | General anchor |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Evidence shows a substantial mismatch or barrier under current conditions. |
| 1 | Weak alignment; substantial gaps or tradeoffs. |
| 2 | Mixed alignment; plausible with meaningful development or compromise. |
| 3 | Strong alignment; relevant evidence with manageable gaps. |
| 4 | Very strong alignment; clear evidence and few material gaps. |
| U | Not enough information. Name what would resolve it. |

For skills and results, 4 means direct, relevant proof; 3 can include strong transferable proof; 2 means partial proof requiring development; 1 means limited evidence after clarification; 0 requires a supported mismatch. An omitted resume bullet alone warrants a question or U, not an automatic zero. For preferences, use the person's stated priorities. For entry requirements, verify jurisdiction and distinguish mandatory conditions from employer preferences.

## Career-path rubric

Assess a reasonably specific direction and entry route, not an entire broad industry. Example: identify the target work and setting before scoring “healthcare.”

| Dimension | Weight | Evidence to assess |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Interest in actual tasks | 20 | Stated preferences, prior experience of tasks, and experiment feedback. |
| Work conditions and values | 15 | Schedule, environment, pace, autonomy, mission, and work to avoid. |
| Economic viability | 15 | Regional entry pay, benefits, transition costs, and the person's floor. |
| Longer-term direction | 10 | The opportunities and development the user wants. |
| Transferable strengths | 15 | Concrete examples relevant to core tasks. |
| Entry route feasibility | 15 | Credentials, hiring requirements, intermediate roles, and access to experience. |
| Transition capacity | 10 | Time, budget, learning requirements, and acceptable pay disruption. |

Show **desirability /60** for the first four dimensions and **transition feasibility /40** for the final three. Apply the unknown rules to each subtotal. A desirable path with low current feasibility may become a longer-term experiment rather than a rejection.

For every path show tasks, evidence, enjoyment assumptions, gaps, mandatory requirements, entry route, current-market sources, and one affordable test. Avoid predicting a person's aptitude from background alone.

## Job-opportunity rubric

| Dimension | Weight | Evidence to assess |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Responsibilities and skills | 25 | Core tasks and required capabilities, including transferable skills. |
| Relevant results and evidence | 20 | Specific accomplishments or work samples supporting the hiring need. |
| Scope and seniority | 15 | Ownership, complexity, technical or supervisory expectations. |
| Career direction | 15 | Interest in actual work and movement toward the chosen goal. |
| Compensation | 10 | Confirmed pay range, likely placement uncertainty, benefits, hours, and pay structure. |
| Work arrangement | 10 | Location, commute, schedule, travel, and other user-stated requirements. |
| Other stated priorities | 5 | Explicit priorities such as sector, stability, values, or learning. |

Show **readiness /60** for the first three dimensions and **desirability /40** for the rest. Do not reward only title matches, employer prestige, or identical industry experience. Explain whether a gap is missing evidence, wording, transferable experience, developable skill, or mandatory eligibility.

## Gates and recommendations

Maintain a separate gate table: requirement; candidate/job evidence; status (Pass, Fail, Unknown); consequence; next verification. A confirmed nonnegotiable failure overrides an enthusiastic recommendation regardless of total. An unknown gate triggers clarification. Legal or licensing eligibility does not become optional because the user likes the role.

Recommendations should reflect the subtotals, gates, user goals, uncertainty, and effort required. Do not apply automatic universal score cutoffs.

- **Prioritize:** Compelling fit with supported evidence and no unresolved critical barrier.
- **Explore/test:** Attractive direction with uncertain enjoyment or developable gaps; test it first.
- **Clarify first:** Missing information could materially change the decision.
- **Longer-term route:** Promising direction with substantial prerequisites or resource needs.
- **Pass/deprioritize:** Confirmed mismatch, unacceptable tradeoff, or weak relevance to goals.

Always give the strongest evidence, material gaps, what could change the assessment, and one next action. Allow the user to pursue a lower-scoring option and explain its tradeoffs honestly.
