# Adaptive intake

Use this as a question bank, not a questionnaire to present in full. Read what the user already supplied. Ask two or three connected questions per turn and summarize useful findings as you go.

## Opening

“We can focus on advancing in similar work, changing fields, or exploring what might fit. Which is closest to where you are? Share your current resume if you have one, or give me a quick overview of your experience.”

If the user has a deadline, ask what is due and address that first.

## Experience and evidence

- What work, education, training, projects, or other responsibilities have you had?
- Which two or three contributions are you proudest of? What did you personally do, who benefited, and what changed?
- What do others regularly rely on you for?
- What important work is absent from your resume or hidden behind your title?
- Which credentials are current, expired, in progress, or required for your target?

Ask for scale or numbers only when useful. Observable outcomes, improved reliability, service quality, safety, learning, and work samples can be valuable evidence. Do not require revenue metrics.

## Interests and reasons for change

- Which tasks give you energy, and which would you prefer less of?
- Describe a good workday and a difficult one. What made the difference?
- Are you trying to change the work itself, your employer, working conditions, or a combination?
- Are there fields you are curious about or would rule out? What attracts or concerns you?
- Would you prefer deeper expertise, managing people, a different setting, more independence, or something else?

Avoid treating interests alone as proof of occupational fit. Clarify the underlying tasks and environment.

## Practical requirements

- What country/region are you targeting? What location, commute, schedule, travel, or physical task requirements matter?
- What pay floor and target should we use? Specify currency, hours, base versus variable pay, and important benefits. Current salary is optional.
- How soon do you need income or a change? Is this active searching or longer-term exploration?
- What time and budget could you realistically put toward training or testing options?
- Would a temporary pay reduction or intermediate role be possible? It is fine if the answer is no.
- Are there other nonnegotiable conditions you want the plan to respect? You can state a work requirement without disclosing personal details.

Ask about authorization to work or credential recognition only when relevant to the market or role; avoid requesting identification numbers or documents.

## Close the initial intake

Produce a brief covering known history, strengths with evidence, interests, work to avoid, goals, requirements, preferences, timing, transition capacity, and unknowns. Separate confirmed facts from tentative interpretations.

Ask: “What would you correct or add? Which requirements are absolute, and which have room for tradeoffs?”

Do not repeat answered questions. For an undecided person, explore paths next. For a decided person, proceed directly to target roles or the job they supplied. Capture writing preferences when drafting becomes relevant.
